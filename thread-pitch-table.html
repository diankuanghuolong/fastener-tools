Choosing coarse vs fine thread

  • Coarse (default): stronger in pull-out for a given length, faster to tap, more resistant to cross-threading and damage. Use it unless you have a specific reason not to.
  • Fine: finer adjustment, higher vibration resistance (less self-loosening), thinner walls OK (smaller minor diameter), better in brittle or thin materials.
  • Example: wheel studs are often M12×1.25 or M14×1.5 fine — more clamping turns in a short hub thickness.

FAQ

What is the tap drill for M8?

6.8 mm for coarse M8×1.25 (≈75% thread), 7.0 mm for fine M8×1. The rule of thumb is drill = diameter − pitch: 8 − 1.25 = 6.75 ≈ 6.8 mm.

How much thread engagement do I need?

Rule of thumb for steel into steel: 1× diameter (M8 → 8 mm engaged). Aluminum or cast iron: 1.5–2×. Plastic: 2–3×. Shorter engagement in harder materials risks stripping the female thread.

My tap keeps breaking — what now?

Check three things: correct drill size for the thread %, appropriate tap type (taper vs plug for through holes), and chip clearing — peck the tap, use tapping fluid, and in blind holes back off every 1–2 turns to break the chip.
