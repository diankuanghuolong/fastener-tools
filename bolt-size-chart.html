How to read the chart

  • Across flats (s) — the width of the hex head from flat to flat; this is your wrench/spanner size.
  • Head height (k) — overall height of the hex head, useful for clearance checks.
  • Tensile stress area (As) — the effective threaded cross-section used in strength and torque calculations (not the shank area πd²/4).
  • Coarse vs fine — coarse is the default (stronger in shear, less sensitive to damage); fine is used where vibration resistance or fine adjustment matters (M10×1.25 wheel studs, for example).

FAQ

What wrench size is an M10 bolt?

17 mm across flats for standard hex heads — this is the "M10" row in the chart. Some high-strength or flange-head fasteners use 15 mm or 16 mm heads; always measure the head if the marking is unreadable.

Metric vs UNC — which is which on a drawing?

Thread callouts tell you: "M10×1.5" is metric (10 mm major diameter, 1.5 mm pitch). "1/2-13 UNC" is imperial (1/2" major diameter, 13 threads per inch). See the converter for common substitutions when the exact size isn't available.

Are DIN and ISO bolts interchangeable?

Dimensionally yes for the same size and grade — DIN 933 and ISO 4017 define the same part. Confirm grade markings (8.8, 10.9 …) match the specification on your drawing, since strength class matters more than the standard's name.
