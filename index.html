Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate bolt torque?

T = K × F × d. K is the torque coefficient (0.20 dry, 0.15 lightly oiled, 0.12 with moly/anti-seize), F the target preload force in newtons, d the nominal diameter in metres. Preload is commonly 75% of proof strength × tensile stress area for reusable joints. Our calculator does this for you.

What is the difference between DIN 933 and ISO 4017?

None in dimensions — they are national/international versions of the same fully threaded hex bolt. DIN 933 = ISO 4017 = GB/T 5783. For partially threaded bolts: DIN 931 = ISO 4014 = GB/T 5782. See the full cross-reference.

What does property class 8.8 mean?

Minimum tensile strength 800 MPa, minimum yield strength (Rp0.2) 640 MPa per ISO 898-1. First digit = 1/100 of tensile strength; second digit = 10 × (yield ÷ tensile). So 10.9 = 1000 MPa tensile, 90% ratio (900 MPa yield).

Which torque factor K should I use?

Plain as-received steel bolts: 0.20. Lightly oiled: 0.15. Coated with molybdenum disulphide or anti-seize: 0.12. Galvanized and other plated fasteners fall between — when in doubt, measure with a torque wrench on a test joint.

Are these charts official standard values?

They are compiled from published ISO/DIN data (ISO 898-1, ISO 4017/4014, ISO 6892) for reference. For contractual or certification purposes, purchase the official standard text.
